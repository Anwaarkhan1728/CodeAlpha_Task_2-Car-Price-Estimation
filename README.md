**Predictive Modelling for Used Car Price Estimation Using Machine Learning: A Comprehensive Data-Driven Approach**

**CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION**

The rapid expansion of digital marketplaces and online automotive platforms has created an urgent requirement for reliable systems capable of predicting the selling price of used cars. Traditionally, this valuation process relied heavily on the judgment of dealers or the negotiation skills of buyers and sellers. Such subjective approaches often lead to inconsistencies, biases, and inaccurate pricing decisions. As the used car market continues to grow, the demand for fair, transparent, and data-driven valuation techniques becomes increasingly significant. Buyers need confidence that they are paying a reasonable amount for a vehicle, while sellers need assurance that they are not undervaluing their assets. Dealerships and online platforms likewise depend on accurate pricing mechanisms to maintain customer trust and operational efficiency.
Machine learning offers a powerful solution to these challenges by learning patterns from historical data and generating precise predictions based on empirical relationships between various vehicle features. This study investigates how data-driven modeling can effectively estimate used car prices and enhance pricing strategies within the automobile industry. The research focuses on analyzing a real-world dataset consisting of multiple car attributes, performing a detailed exploratory data analysis to understand underlying patterns, engineering new features such as car age and brand, and building several machine learning models to determine the most accurate predictor. The primary objective is to construct a predictive model that performs consistently across different types of cars and represents a reliable framework for decision-making in real-world scenarios.

**CHAPTER 2
LITERATURE REVIEW**

The problem of predicting used car prices has been widely studied within the fields of machine learning, econometrics, and automotive analytics. Previous work highlights that car price is influenced by a combination of numerical variables, such as present showroom price, kilometers driven, vehicle age, and owner records, as well as categorical variables, including fuel type, transmission mode, and brand reputation. Traditional regression models like Linear Regression have been used to capture basic relationships, but they often fail to model complex non-linear patterns inherent in real-world data. Such limitations motivated researchers to explore tree-based approaches such as Decision Trees, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting, which handle interactions more effectively and offer improved accuracy.
Studies consistently show that Random Forest and Gradient Boosting outperform classical regression techniques due to their ability to capture feature interactions, manage outliers, and reduce overfitting. Literature also emphasizes the importance of feature engineering, particularly transforming year into car age and extracting brand names, which significantly enhances model learning. Exploratory data analysis emerges as an essential step in nearly all research, as it reveals underlying trends, identifies skewed distributions, and highlights correlations that assist in model development. This study builds upon the strengths of previous research while introducing a structured and comprehensive modeling framework tailored specifically for used car price prediction.

**CHAPTER 3
METHODOLOGY**

This research follows a systematic methodology beginning with data acquisition and preprocessing, followed by exploratory data analysis, feature engineering, model training, and final evaluation. The dataset used in this study contains 301 observations and nine attributes, including car name, manufacturing year, present price, selling price, kilometers driven, fuel type, selling type, transmission, and ownership history. Initial inspection confirms that the dataset contains no missing values, ensuring smooth processing. To better reflect depreciation patterns, the manufacturing year is converted into car age by subtracting the year value from 2026. Car names are further processed to extract brand names, acknowledging the significant impact brand reputation has on resale value.
Exploratory data analysis provides deep insights into variable behavior. The selling price distribution is found to be right-skewed, with most used cars priced under ten lakhs, although luxury vehicles create a heavy upper tail. Correlation analysis reveals that the present showroom price has a strong positive relationship with selling price, while car age shows a negative correlation, confirming real-world depreciation effects. Categorically, diesel vehicles tend to receive higher resale values than petrol cars, and automatic transmission models sustain slightly higher prices compared to manual ones. Dealer selling types also exhibit marginally higher pricing than individual sellers.
Data preprocessing is carried out using a combined pipeline. Numerical features are standardized using StandardScaler to ensure uniform scaling, while categorical features undergo OneHotEncoding to convert them into machine-readable form. A ColumnTransformer organizes these operations efficiently. The dataset is then divided into training and testing sets using an 80:20 ratio. Five machine learning models, Linear Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and Support Vector Regression, are trained through pipelines that ensure proper preprocessing during model fitting and evaluation. This integrated pipeline prevents data leakage and ensures reliable performance comparison between models.

**CHAPTER 4
RESULTS**

The performance of each trained model is evaluated using the R² score, mean absolute error, and root mean square error. Linear Regression produces moderate accuracy but struggles to model the non-linearity present in the dataset. The Decision Tree model performs better but shows signs of overfitting, especially when predicting extreme car prices. The Gradient Boosting model demonstrates high accuracy and stable predictions. However, among all models tested, the Random Forest Regressor delivers the most impressive results, achieving an R² score of 0.9651, indicating excellent alignment between predicted and actual values.
A closer examination of the Random Forest model reveals strong predictive reliability. The actual versus predicted plot shows a tight clustering of points around the diagonal line, verifying high precision. The residual distribution appears symmetrical and centered around zero, confirming that the model exhibits no systemic bias. Feature importance analysis identifies the present showroom price as the most influential variable in determining resale price, followed by car age and brand. Fuel type, selling type, and transmission show moderate influence, aligning with real-world automotive pricing trends. Although cross-validation reveals one fold with extreme variation, likely due to the small dataset size and outliers, the overall mean score supports the robustness of the model.

**CHAPTER 5
DISCUSSION**

The superior performance of the Random Forest model can be attributed to its ensemble nature, which constructs multiple decision trees and aggregates their predictions to reduce variance and improve generalization. This enables the model to capture complex interactions between car attributes and manage non-linear relationships effectively. The study’s findings confirm established automotive pricing logic: cars with higher initial showroom prices naturally retain a greater portion of their value over time; lower car age corresponds to higher resale value due to reduced wear and depreciation; and brand reputation significantly influences how much a car can command in the second-hand market.
The research also highlights the impact of fuel type and transmission on pricing, with diesel and automatic vehicles generally showing higher resale values. The presence of luxury cars introduces price variation but does not diminish model performance, as Random Forest handles outliers effectively. These insights collectively demonstrate the importance of combining statistical analysis, domain knowledge, and machine learning techniques to develop accurate prediction systems. The approach used in this study is not only effective for the given dataset but also scalable for larger datasets used by dealerships, online car platforms, and vehicle inspection companies.

**CHAPTER 6
CONCLUSION**

This study successfully demonstrates that machine learning provides an efficient and accurate mechanism for predicting used car prices. Through a structured workflow involving exploratory data analysis, feature engineering, preprocessing, model training, and performance evaluation, the Random Forest Regressor emerges as the most reliable model. With an R² value of 0.9651, its predictions strongly align with actual selling prices, confirming its suitability for real-world deployment. The integration of feature importance analysis also enhances interpretability, providing users with valuable insights into what drives vehicle resale value. Overall, the research presents a complete modeling framework that can significantly improve transparency and fairness within the used car market.

**CHAPTER 7
FUTURE WORK**

Despite the strong results achieved, this study opens several opportunities for further research. Expanding the dataset to include more vehicles from different regions and market segments would allow the model to generalize more effectively. Adding features such as accident history, service records, car condition scores, insurance claims, and geographical pricing trends could significantly enhance accuracy. Exploring advanced techniques such as deep neural networks or hybrid ensemble frameworks may further improve predictive performance, especially on large and diverse datasets. Finally, deploying this model through a web application or a mobile interface would provide real-time access to users, enabling customers and dealers to instantly obtain fair and accurate price estimates.
